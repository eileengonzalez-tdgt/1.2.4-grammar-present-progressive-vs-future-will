With a partner, find the answers in the calendar.

  1. Who has a Thai massage on Wednesday at 9:15 a.m.?
  2. What is Jake Willis doing on Thursday at 10:00 a.m.?
  3. When is Celeste King having her appointment?
  4. Who has an appointment on Friday at 10:15 a.m.?

Part B · Discover the difference

Arrangement: “Marina is having a Thai massage on Wednesday at 9:15 a.m.”

Prediction: “I think Marina will enjoy her massage.”

Discuss: Which statement is supported by the calendar? Which expresses an opinion about the future? What verb pattern changes?

Present Progressive describes a future arrangement. Will expresses a prediction about the result.

Part C · Create Two Sentences

Now use the pattern you discovered. Write one confirmed arrangement based on the calendar and one prediction about that appointment. Use complete sentences.

Arrangement: Teagan Warren is having a Thai massage on Friday at 9:15 a.m.

Prediction: I think Teagan will feel relaxed afterward.

Other correct answers are possible. Your trainer can review your sentences.

Step 2 of 6

Clarify · Choose the Right Future Form

Focus on what the speaker means, not just words such as tomorrow or next week.

1 · Present Progressive: arranged future plans

Form: subject + am/is/are + verb-ing + future time expression.

“I’m meeting the recruiter on Wednesday.” · “We’re having dinner on Friday.”

Use it when a future activity is already arranged, often with a time, place, or another person. The same form also describes actions happening now, so context matters.

2 · Will: predictions, instant decisions, offers, promises

Form: subject + will + base verb. Negative: won’t + base verb.

  • Prediction: “I think the event will be fun.”
  • Spontaneous decision: “The phone is ringing. I’ll answer it.”
  • Offer: “I’ll help you set up the chairs.”
  • Promise: “I won’t forget to send the invitation.”

Important: Will does not always mean the event is certain. Predictions can be uncertain.

3 · Statements, negatives, and questions

FormPresent ProgressiveWill
AffirmativeShe is meeting a client tomorrow.She will call later.
NegativeShe isn’t meeting a client tomorrow.She won’t call later.
QuestionIs she meeting a client tomorrow?Will she call later?

Future time phrases include tomorrow, next week, on Friday, tonight. They can occur with either form.

4 · Avoid these common mistakes

✗ I will meeting you tomorrow. → ✓ I will meet you tomorrow.

✗ She is meet the manager tomorrow. → ✓ She is meeting the manager tomorrow.

✗ Will you coming? → ✓ Will you come?

Extra note: Be going to also expresses intentions and predictions, but today's main comparison is Present Progressive vs. will.

Step 4 of 6

Internalize · My Plans, Your Predictions

In pairs, alternate between describing confirmed plans and responding naturally.

Student A · Share three arrangements

Use Present Progressive to describe three confirmed plans. Include a day or time.

Prompts: Who are you meeting? Where are you going? What are you doing this weekend?

“I’m visiting Antigua on Saturday.”

Student B · Respond with will

Make a prediction, offer help, or make a spontaneous decision. Then ask a follow-up question.

“I think you’ll have a great time! Are you staying overnight?”

Swap roles after several exchanges.

Speaking support

Arrangements: I’m meeting… · We’re visiting… · I’m having…

Responses: I think you’ll… · I’ll help you… · Don’t worry, I’ll…

Follow-ups: What time? · Who with? · Where are you meeting? · Why do you think so?

Timing: 1 minute to prepare, 8 minutes of partner exchanges, 1 minute of feedback.

Step 5 of 6

Fluent Use · Future Newsroom

In groups of 3–4, deliver a short news report about upcoming events and what might happen.

Your news report

Choose a topic: a community event, an upcoming workplace activity, or a fictional news story.

  1. Announce two confirmed arrangements using Present Progressive.
  2. Make two predictions using will.
  3. Include one natural offer or spontaneous decision while preparing or responding.
  4. Give each group member a speaking role.

Sample opening

“Good afternoon! Our community is holding a block party this Saturday. Sarah is bringing refreshments, and James is managing the sound system. We think the event will attract lots of neighbors, and it will be a great opportunity to meet people.”

Remember: use the Present Progressive for arrangements and will for predictions. If someone needs help during preparation, respond naturally: “I’ll help you!”

Timing: 3 minutes to prepare, 5 minutes for mini-reports, 2 minutes for feedback. If recording is requested, follow your trainer’s delivery instructions.
